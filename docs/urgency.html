The board

Urgency

A score decides what comes first. It is the sum of three things, each shown on the board.

the formula
urgency = (100 − rule priority) + waiting + ticket

The three parts

PartPointsFrom
Rule100 − priorityThe rule that lists the task. A review to do (priority 10) gives 90; a draft to finish (priority 90) gives 10.
Waiting3 per day, at most 60The full days since the next step arrived: since the review was requested, since the oldest unanswered reply, since your last word when you wait on someone.
Ticket+5, plus the priorityA change that names a ticket in its title or branch (PROJ-12) gets 5, plus the points of the ticket's priority when Jira can tell it. A ticket gets the points of its own priority.
Ticket priorityPoints
Highest+50
High+30
Medium+10
Low0
Lowest−10

An example

i on a task
HOW ITS URGENCY ADDS UP
rule          80  "Re-review" has priority 20 (0 first, 100 last): 100 − 20
waiting      +15  5 days × 3 a day
ticket       +35  tied to PROJ-440, "high" (+30), and +5 for naming a ticket
urgency    = 130  hot: warm ▴ from 100, hot ▲ from 130

Where the board shows it

  • The detail pane sums it up: urgency 130 = rule 80, waiting +15, PROJ-440 (high) +35.
  • Panes ordered by urgency show the score on every line.
  • i on a task lays it out line by line, with your own weights.
  • ▴ marks a task from 100 points (warm), ▲ from 130 (hot). The filter heat:hot,warm keeps them.

Orders

Panes list the most urgent first by default. s changes a pane's order and v reverses it.

OrderFirst
urgencyThe highest score; among equals, the one that has waited longest.
ruleThe rules' own order; among equals, the freshest.
waitingWhat has waited longest.
updatedWhat moved most recently.
createdWhat was opened first.

Change the weights

~/.config/aki/config.toml
[urgency]
per_day = 3
waiting_cap = 60
linked = 5
highest = 50
high = 30
medium = 10
low = 0
lowest = -10
warm = 100
hot = 130

The rule's part follows the rule's priority: change it under [rules.<name>]. aki rules prints the weights in use.

How a change is tied to a ticket

By a key in its title or branch name, such as PROJ-12. With a Jira source configured, the key is looked up, even for tickets that are not yours, and its priority counts. Without one, anything shaped like a key counts as a ticket and gets the 5 points.
